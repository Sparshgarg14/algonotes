## Problem

You are given an array of length `2n` containing exactly two zeros and numbers from `0` to `n`. Find the longest palindromic subarray that contains both zeros, and return the **smallest missing positive integer** from it.

## The Core Insight

The key observation is about **where the center of any palindrome containing both zeros must lie**.

Call the positions of the two zeros `x` and `y` (where `x < y`).

For any palindrome containing both zeros, the zeros must be **symmetrically placed** about the center. This gives us exactly **3 candidate centers** to check:

<Callout type="info">
  You don't need to check all possible centers — the two zeros themselves constrain where the palindrome center must be. Only 3 positions are ever worth checking.
</Callout>

### The 3 Candidate Centers

**Center 1 — Zero x is the center**

The palindrome is centered at position `x` itself (odd-length palindrome expanding from `x`).

**Center 2 — Zero y is the center**

The palindrome is centered at position `y` itself.

**Center 3 — The midpoint between x and y**

If both zeros have the same parity of index (both even or both odd), their midpoint is a single integer — one center exists between them.

If they have opposite parity, the midpoint falls between two indices — meaning a **two-character center** (even-length palindrome). The two centers are `(x+y)/2` and `(x+y+1)/2` — which are always adjacent to each other.
